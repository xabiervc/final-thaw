# FINAL THAW — Story Bible

## Logline

In 2089, while climate collapse fractures the world, a methodical atmospheric scientist and a disillusioned former police officer race toward the last working atmospheric relay. The device they carry may save humanity, but the corporation hunting them wants to decide who counts as humanity.

## Themes

### Survival is political
The crisis is physical, but access to survival infrastructure is controlled. The conflict is not whether technology can help; it is who controls it and who is excluded.

### Cooperation precedes trust
Elena and Marcus do not become friends because the plot demands it. They cooperate because neither can complete the route alone. Trust grows from repeated, observable choices.

### Repair is not restoration
The Aster Protocol does not undo the past or instantly heal the world. It buys time, slows collapse, and makes collective rebuilding possible.

### Consequences must be legible
The story does not punish players with secret moral arithmetic. Aid, protection, integrity, and evidence are visible and tied to clear outcomes.

## World State: 2089

Climate instability is geographically uneven and violent: flash floods, fire fronts, storm surges, heat domes, crop failures, and infrastructure collapse overlap. States still exist, but emergency power is fragmented. The Helix Consortium controls much of the practical infrastructure: grids, logistics, food routing, emergency contracts, and private security.

The game does not depict ordinary people as a hostile mass. Scavengers, evacuees, shelter residents, workers, and port communities respond differently to scarcity and coercion. Violence is contextual, not innate.

## The Aster Protocol

A field-deployable atmospheric stabilisation method developed by the Earth Systems Restoration Network. It can reduce the cascading feedback loop that makes storms, heat, and water systems increasingly unstable.

It cannot return the climate to a previous baseline. It needs a functioning relay—Final Thaw Station—to reach sufficient scale. It is consequential because it can be copied, distributed, and politically controlled.

## Helix Consortium

Helix is a coalition of infrastructure and energy corporations. Its public language is technical and reassuring: continuity, prioritisation, protected zones, managed recovery. Its private strategy is selective survival: reserve working systems for communities that can be controlled, productive, or purchased.

Helix should never be written as magically omnipotent. It wins through access to logistics, surveillance, emergency authority, and the exhaustion of institutions—not through implausible omniscience.

## Narrative Rules

- Do not use climate collapse as spectacle alone. Each crisis must change movement, resources, social behavior, or level logic.
- Do not reduce Elena to a passive objective. Her systems knowledge repeatedly changes what is possible.
- Do not reduce Marcus to a violent protector. His history with policing and emergency enforcement creates moral tension and specific knowledge of Helix tactics.
- Do not resolve institutional wrongdoing through the death of one villain alone.
- Keep dialogue compact; environmental storytelling carries a substantial share of context.

## Act I: Separation

Elena escapes a failing monitoring facility with the Aster prototype. She learns that Helix delayed deployment of viable stabilisation research because it could not monopolise its distribution.

Marcus begins under an extraction order. During riots, highway fights, and the shelter blockade, he witnesses Helix treating displaced people as obstacles. He discovers the order has changed: if Elena cannot be captured, she is to be eliminated.

They meet in the flooded shelter. Elena reads Marcus as Helix personnel. Marcus has not yet earned trust, but warns her honestly. They escape together into toxic rain.

**Act I question:** Can cooperation begin before trust exists?

## Act II: Cooperation

Inside the damaged vehicle, Elena recalibrates Aster and identifies Final Thaw Station as the only relay capable of planetary-scale deployment.

Elena crosses a collapsing dam, proving that systems knowledge is practical survival rather than abstract expertise. Marcus crosses the port, where evacuation infrastructure is deliberately withheld. Both routes show Helix's selective survival model from different angles.

At the mountain transit hub, the player first switches freely. Elena and Marcus must coordinate. Their disagreement becomes explicit: Elena prioritises deployment even if Helix might seize the result; Marcus argues that a stable climate under Helix control preserves a different catastrophe.

**Act II question:** Is saving the system enough if the system remains unjust?

## Act III: The Final Thaw

At Final Thaw Station, Helix occupies the relay. The pair finds evidence that Helix engineered the apparent failure of an earlier Aster test to justify emergency authority and suppress public deployment.

The player makes the final evidence choice: preserve it and risk delay, or erase it to prioritise immediate activation. This choice affects the political meaning of the ending, not whether the game can be completed.

The Helix Retrieval Commander tries to secure the prototype. The final battle operationalises the protagonists' partnership: Elena calibrates the relay; Marcus creates the conditions for her to do so.

**Act III question:** What does a real recovery require beyond technical survival?

## Endings

### Public Thaw
Aster activates with sufficient integrity. The evidence survives and civilians have been aided. Distribution data is released publicly; Helix loses legitimacy and cannot fully monopolise recovery. The future remains difficult, but survival becomes a shared project.

### Guarded Thaw
Aster works, but Helix retains control of the distribution narrative or practical access. Weather destabilisation slows. Protected zones improve first; outsiders remain dependent. This ending is stable but morally unresolved.

### Fragile Thaw
The relay activates imperfectly due to serious prototype damage or low Elena Safety. The feedback loop weakens but remains dangerous. Recovery is still possible, but all actors face narrower margins and harder compromises.

## Canon Facts

- The game occurs in 2089.
- Aster is a stabilisation protocol, not a time machine or universal cure.
- Final Thaw Station is a mountain atmospheric relay.
- Helix delayed/sabotaged earlier public Aster deployment to protect control over recovery infrastructure.
- Elena developed Aster with a wider research network; she did not invent climate science alone.
- Marcus has prior experience with emergency enforcement and has participated in systems he now rejects.
- The story has no canonical ending; each ending is valid based on player state.
